01 / The idea

Science is accelerating.
Let’s help it keep up.

AI is transforming research, from generating hypotheses to running experiments and making discoveries. As scientific output grows, our ability to evaluate, validate, and distinguish meaningful discoveries from noise needs to grow with it.

This hackathon brings together Stockholm AI, the AI for Meta-Science NeurIPS Workshop, and Sweden AI Factory to explore better ways of doing science.

02 / The challenges

Three tasks. Real research problems.

Full task brief PDF ↓

The brief outlines three candidate tasks, from the first research question to the evidence behind a published claim. Open a task to explore what to build.

01Ideation stress-test toolIs this question worth asking?

Researchers can spend months on a question that is vague, impossible to answer with available data, or already answered by prior work. These problems are often caught late. Build an early check on whether a question is precise, testable, and worth the resources.

What to build

A tool that takes one research question and:

  • Extracts and restates its key terms, flagging ambiguity or inconsistent use in the literature.
  • Surfaces the question’s implicit assumptions.
  • Identifies the evidence or data needed to answer it, and flags evidence that is unlikely to be obtainable.
  • Recommends whether to proceed, narrow, revise, or stop, with reasoning.

What success looks like

A vague or overly broad question gets a specific, actionable critique. For example, identify the three different meanings of “engagement” in the literature, rather than simply suggesting that the researcher clarify their terms.

02Provenance-preserving lab notebookKeep the research trail intact.

Good research leaves a trail: why a method was chosen, what was abandoned, and which code produced which result. When AI tools rapidly iterate on research tasks, that trail can disappear, eroding accountability and reproducibility.

What to build

A lightweight logging or wrapper tool around a single AI-assisted research task, such as data cleaning, running experiments, or iterating on a model. Automatically capture:

  • Each proposed plan or hypothesis and the reasoning behind it.
  • What was actually run, including code, parameters, and data versions.
  • What failed or contradicted expectations, and how the plan changed in response.
  • A timeline a human can follow to reconstruct the actual decision path.

What success looks like

A reviewer who wasn’t present can inspect the log and answer “Why did they change approach at step 4?” without asking the researcher.

03Claim-to-evidence linkerFollow the claim. Find the evidence.

Reviewers rarely have time to trace every claim to its supporting evidence. Polished writing can make a paper feel convincing without making its claims well supported. Build a way to inspect those links directly.

What to build

A tool that takes one paper or preprint, identifies its major claims, and links each to its exact supporting figure, table, results paragraph, or code path. Flag:

  • Claims with no traceable supporting evidence in the document.
  • Evidence that is weaker than the claim’s stated confidence, such as a strong conclusion backed only by a single small-sample experiment.
  • What was checked and what remains unverified.

The output

An inspectable map of claim → evidence → confidence or caveat, so readers can assess each link individually.
